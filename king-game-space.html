Case 05 / Future Platform Strategy

Game Space Strategic Initiative

Principal UX Designer | Strategic Product Discovery | 2023-Present

I explored a future-facing platform concept that organized internal tools around the game team context: environments, applications, ownership, operations, and the work needed to keep a live game moving.

Strategic Problem

Platform tools were powerful, but users still had to assemble context across products. The experience did not yet reflect the way game teams think about their work.

Design Goal

Create a concept direction for a game-centered workspace that could align teams around a clearer platform north star.

Why it mattered

Artifact slot / Game Space concept screens, scenario prototype, target operating model, or dashboard modal

The platform was improving feature by feature, but the broader experience still required users to know where every capability lived. Game teams needed a more natural home base: a place to understand what belongs to a game, what state it is in, who owns it, and what actions are available.

Game Space was the strategic exploration that asked: what would the internal platform look like if the primary organizing unit was the game team's actual work, not the platform's technical architecture?

What I led

Opportunity framing

I synthesized pain points from platform workstreams into a clearer strategic opportunity around game-centered context.

Concept modeling

I explored how tools, environments, applications, ownership, and operational states could be represented in one connected workspace.

Scenario prototyping

I used scenarios to test whether the concept supported real user tasks, not just a cleaner information architecture.

Cross-functional alignment

The concept helped PM and engineering partners discuss future platform direction through a concrete product vision.

Concept direction

1. Put the game at the center

Instead of making users navigate from tool to tool, the workspace starts from the game context and reveals relevant environments, applications, ownership, and status.

2. Connect operational signals

The concept brings scattered states into one surface so users can understand what is healthy, what needs attention, and which system or owner is involved.

3. Make platform relationships visible

Internal platforms often hide dependencies until something breaks. The concept exposes relationships between tools, teams, and environments earlier.

4. Preserve expert depth

The goal was not to flatten every tool into one dashboard. It was to create a better home base that points users to the right detail at the right time.

Validation and next steps

The concept was used as a strategic artifact: a way to align teams, test assumptions, and discuss where platform experience should move next. The value was in making an abstract platform strategy visible enough to critique.

  • Validated whether the game-centered model matched how teams talk about their work.
  • Identified which cross-tool relationships mattered most for day-to-day operations.
  • Separated near-term navigation improvements from longer-term platform workspace opportunities.
The design shifted the conversation from "which tool owns this?" to "what does a game team need to understand and act with confidence?"

Impact

  • Created a concrete product vision for a more integrated internal platform experience.
  • Helped connect tactical feature work to a larger strategy for game-team self-service and operational clarity.
  • Provided a shared artifact for roadmap conversations across product, engineering, and UX.
