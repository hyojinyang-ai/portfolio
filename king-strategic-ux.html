King: Driving Strategic UX Design and Maturity

Principal UX Designer | King (Activision Blizzard) | 2023-Present

This work sits above a single feature launch. I partnered across Core Platform and internal tooling teams to turn fragmented technical systems into clearer product experiences, while raising the UX maturity of a highly engineering-led platform organization.

Context

Core Platform supported game operations, developer workflows, internal applications, and shared services used across multiple King titles.

Design Mandate

Move UX from reactive screen delivery into strategic product shaping, evidence-led decision making, and reusable platform patterns.

Key challenges

Low UX maturity

UX was often brought in after technical decisions were already shaped. I needed to build influence through useful artifacts, evidence, and practical delivery.

Fragmented ownership

Internal platform experiences crossed many teams, systems, and permission models. Users felt the fragmentation even when the org chart explained it.

Scattered evidence

Research findings, design decisions, and rationale were not easy to find or reuse, which slowed alignment and repeated earlier debates.

Strategic ambiguity

The platform needed a clearer product direction, not only interface improvements. Design had to connect day-to-day usability work with long-term strategy.

What I led

  • Defined UX strategy across multiple internal platform workstreams and connected tactical delivery to platform-level outcomes.
  • Facilitated discovery, stakeholder alignment, journey mapping, and cross-functional decision-making with PM, engineering, and support partners.
  • Created reusable research and design documentation practices so teams could work from shared evidence instead of scattered memory.
  • Translated complex technical workflows into clearer product models, self-service flows, navigation patterns, and future-state concepts.

Key takeaway

The strongest contribution was not only improving screens. It was creating the conditions for better platform decisions: clearer user evidence, reusable design patterns, shared language, and strategic UX presence in spaces where design had previously been underrepresented.
